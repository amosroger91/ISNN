# Build Overtime Desk into a broadcast

**Implementation owner: Grok. Editorial and channel owner: Isaac.** Build a 12–20 minute multi-sport recap that airs at **8:00 a.m. Central seven days a week**, with **one independently edited YouTube Short per segment** released at staggered times later that day. The morning episode is the canonical recap. Scan the full configured sports slate, lead with the biggest verified stories, and use real game clips to explain decisive plays where cleared. Give Isaac an on-demand **Check trend** tool and a daily trend desk focused on the episode’s core story. Start with NBA as the first structured data integration and one adult independent or regional league as the first footage pilot. Use the React, Express, Canvas, Kokoro and FFmpeg stack for the first cut. A public episode and Shorts package with cleared game action is a separate milestone from shipping the studio. This plan assumes no provider contract, footage or music rights, account access, approved spend, or commitment to an xAI production API. Provider references were reviewed November 15, 2026. Select one adapter after a 7–14 night shadow comparison of game coverage, correction delay, cost and reliability. Confirm in writing the rights for monetized YouTube, computed graphics, archive, cache, attribution and model processing. Sportradar’s published official media addendum specifies non-commercial use and requires approval for new derived data points. The NBA statistics terms allow legitimate news reporting subject to their conditions. Link to primary pages for human checks and use authorized interfaces for automated ingestion. Use RSS and approved searches for discovery. Poll permitted feeds throughout the evening and overnight according to contract, source cache intervals and quotas. Track game state through final and later corrections, including postponed games, overtime and late West Coast finishes. Reconcile from change logs and retain a short replay window when permitted. For link-only sources, retain allowed metadata and a human evidence locator. Deduplicate provider IDs and syndication by original reporter or scorer. Keep provider-neutral adapters so the next beat can use MLB or NFL official evidence and its own commercial feed after coverage and rights review. [NBA change log](https://developer.sportradar.com/basketball/reference/nba-daily-change-log), [MLB scores](https://www.mlb.com/scores), [NFL game books](https://support.nfl.com/hc/en-us/articles/35869678028180-Game-Books).

**Scan across sports from day one.** Seed a configurable desk list for NFL, NBA, MLB, NHL, WNBA, NCAA football and basketball, MLS and major international soccer. Add recurring coverage of other global events as the sources and editorial scope mature. Start each non-integrated beat with official score, schedule and announcement links for human verification, approved RSS where available, and credential-free fixtures and manual entries for development. Record each scan’s time, relevant changes and coverage state (`quiet`, `covered`, `awaiting verification`, or `source unavailable`). This gives Isaac a full slate view and a way to spot missed results even while structured ingestion expands one sport at a time. Say which major beats are missing verification when that affects the audience. Weight genuine consequence and freshness across sports so a densely covered league cannot crowd out the night’s major story elsewhere.

### Measure interest in the core story each day

Give Isaac a **Check trend** action on any pitch, with editable search aliases and an explainable recommendation. For the on-air trend desk, Isaac locks the episode’s core topic as a canonical game, event, transaction, or explicit person/team and story ID. Require a matching ID or a documented link to the same person/team **and** dated story. A shared league or surname is too broad. Isaac can designate a specific secondary core story, with its own scope and approval recorded. For example, “the final possession of last night’s NBA Finals game” can qualify for that game. A same-name celebrity story or a generic ticket-sale spike is off-topic. Run the check at midnight and refresh before lock. Put one or two qualified angles together in a daily trend segment and give that segment one Short. When the check finds no qualifying story, show the reason in the desk and use a brief status line in the roundup if useful. Surface feed failure as `signal unavailable`.

Begin with the US Google Trending Now RSS or CSV export for recent surges. Use active status, timestamps and estimated movement or volume when the selected export includes them. Map query aliases to canonical entities and keep the original observation. Collect two observations at least 15 minutes apart, with the latest qualifying observation within six hours at lock. Optionally corroborate with an entitled X recent-count series for tightly scoped queries excluding reposts, compared with matched time windows on prior days and a minimum baseline. Deduplicate network or publisher spikes. Record `provider`, geography, period, metric and unit, query and aliases, observed and retrieved times, source URL, matched story ID, score version, comparison and explanation. Google Explore’s normalized 0–100 values and platform post counts measure different things. Avoid added raw totals across platforms. Use own-channel YouTube Analytics for retrospective calibration. [Google Trending Now](https://support.google.com/trends/answer/3076011), [Google Trends normalization](https://support.google.com/trends/answer/4365533), [X recent post counts](https://docs.x.com/x-api/posts/counts/introduction), [YouTube Analytics](https://developers.google.com/youtube/analytics/sample-requests).

| Starting pilot scorecard | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Surge **0–40**, persistence **0–25**, independent attention corroboration **0–20**, editorial novelty **0–15** | Normalize within each provider and time window. Show Isaac the individual values and missing signals. Filter promos, repost spikes, homonyms, stale terms and previously covered angles without new evidence. |
| **70–84**, with fresh persistent signal, a verified angle and an in-scope connection | Recommend `daily segment`. An out-of-scope pitch stays outside this episode’s trend segment. |
| **85+**, with a fresh verified angle, at least two independent attention sources and material original analysis or visual evidence | Recommend `standalone video pitch`. Isaac decides whether to develop it. An in-scope angle can also air in the trend segment. The recommendation leaves the 08:00 flagship and assigned Shorts schedule intact. |
| Lower score or incomplete observations | `Watch` for a verified developing pitch. Use `insufficient evidence` for a failed factual check or unverifiable signal, and record its reason. |

Attention measures audience interest. Verify factual claims through the same evidence ledger used for the rest of the show. Show what is newly trending about the story and why that matters. One exact-game query scoring 34 + 21 + 15 + 11 earns a score of 81 and can be pitched for the trend segment. Reject the same-name collision even if it has more search traffic. Calibrate the illustrative thresholds in 14 shadow days using Isaac’s adjudications, false positives and retention. Log any editorial override as such with its author and reason. An override leaves the source-and-rights gates in force.

Give graphics distinct roles and reserve caption space. Use a 5% title-safe inset as a starting point and review at phone size. ## Air the recap at eight and stagger the Shorts

The worker should consume separate resolved horizontal and vertical timelines with frame and sample ranges, caption cues, evidence IDs, and pinned rights, asset and design versions. Cache narration and unaffected shots by input digest. Emit the main `episode.mp4`, captions and thumbnail, plus one vertical MP4 and captions per segment. Write `timeline.json`, `credits.json`, `cue-sheet.json`, `qc.json` and release metadata per item. Check full decode, 1080p H.264 progressive BT.709 SDR with fast start, 48 kHz AAC stereo, A/V sync within 80 ms, measured mix, caption ordering and safe placement. Detect unplanned black, silence and freezes with allowances for authored holds. Measure ten ordinary and busy slates on the existing two-vCPU, 1,800 MB Compose target. Begin with provisional warm targets of a five-minute proxy and a 25-minute P95 full episode encode, with three minutes per Short. Prioritize the flagship render and upload, set cutoffs from measured upload and processing latency, and move heavy work to a separate worker if the show interferes with Bible jobs. Report bytes, provider calls, processor time and cash spend per edition. Retain final packages, approvals and manifests according to grants and policy, and expire temporary proxies and superseded assets on their permitted schedules. [YouTube encoding guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1722171), [FFprobe](https://ffmpeg.org/ffprobe.html), [Compose resource limits](../deploy/docker-compose.yml).

Keep the midnight rundown and set provisional Central deadlines of 06:35 fact reconciliation, 06:45 editorial lock and 07:50 processed private recap. Keep the existing release mode available until an authorized trial succeeds. Private-stage each approved item, attach permitted delivery assets and poll processing status. The 08:00 `America/Chicago` cron publishes the canonical long-form video by promoting its existing video ID once revision, processing, evidence, rights and hold state agree. Verify playable public status and record upload, processing and public timestamps separately. Respect daylight saving time and retain a fixed 08:00 local target on weekends and holidays. Validate OAuth scopes, channel upload caps and API quotas with the proposed daily load of a main episode plus up to seven Shorts. Keep SRTs available for manual track upload in the pilot. [YouTube owner processing](https://developers.google.com/youtube/data/guides/implementation/videos), [Video update scopes](https://developers.google.com/youtube/data/reference/rest/v3/videos/update), [Captions upload](https://developers.google.com/youtube/data/reference/rest/v3/captions/insert).

Plan one slot per actual segment. For the seven-segment example, assign NBA at **10:00**, NFL at **11:30**, MLB at **13:00**, NHL or WNBA at **14:30**, soccer or college at **16:00**, roundup at **17:30**, and core-topic trends at **19:00 Central**. Persist the assignments at lock in the desk and allow Isaac to reorder before a slot. With fewer segments, space releases evenly through the configured window and confirm each assignment. Use configurable spacing or additional authorized windows for a larger edition so every segment still has a distinct slot. The scheduler rechecks that the main video is publicly playable and the exact Short is processed, cleared, current and unheld. Use a small configurable slot grace, then expire a missed item. A delayed flagship suspends its children until it is public and their eligible slots arrive. Missed or expired items stay private, with reasons shown in the desk. The system avoids batch catch-up. After the main video is public, queue an operator task to set it as the Short’s related video in Studio where advanced feature access is available. [YouTube related videos for Shorts](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14075157).

Use one durable `ReleaseItem` per edition and surface, with stable segment ID for Shorts, a revision and manifest digest, intended local slot and UTC instant, eligible time window, canonical private YouTube ID, processing state, hold reason and actual release time. A single publisher lease or atomic status transition protects each scheduled promotion. Main and Shorts have distinct IDs and state. Version changes or a retracted claim invalidate every affected item, even when the main is live and only a Short is still private. Hold the superseded item unless a replacement is cleared before its own expiry. If an update returns an ambiguous result, reconcile that exact ID before retrying. In an incident, pause public automation, preserve the approved cuts and reconcile all staged IDs before resuming. Isaac handles public corrections and rights claims, with independent decisions for cuts already aired.
